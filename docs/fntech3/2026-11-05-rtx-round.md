# RTX path tracer round, 2026-11-05

A round of fixes and speed-ups to RTX's path tracer, on top of
`6cad618cf576b6fdef46ec8e12b5235ff8963360`. Four read-only reviews (the
per-pixel shaders, traversal and denoising, the light and material tables,
and the Vulkan frame and lifetime code) proposed defects; each one below was
traced in the code before it was changed, and the ones not changed are listed
at the end. The [JSON companion](2026-11-05-rtx-round.json) holds the source
and runtime identities, commands and figures.

Every change keeps fnTech3's traced light: the level's own lights, nothing
baked, with FnQ's denoiser, HDR and fog paths (invariant 11). No module ABI,
wire, demo, save, asset or pak contract is touched, and no compatibility gate
closes.

## A still view no longer darkens

**Defect, pre-existing.** With `rtx_rt_accumulate 1` (the default), a view
that stands still averages its frames into a history held in half floats.
On this machine's NVIDIA driver that average fell steadily: Quake III's
`four` demo, frozen, lost a fifth of its brightness over 1,600 frames
(whole-frame luma 127.0, 116.3, 108.0, 101.1 at 400, 800, 1,200 and 1,600
frames) while the raw trace stayed constant (the means of 20 raw frames
1,200 frames apart differ by 0.13%). A history 1,700 samples deep moves less than a half float's
step per sample, and a store that rounds loses part of every move.

**Diagnosis.** The drift was the same with the spatial filter off
(`rtx_rt_denoise_iterations 0`), with the sub-pixel jitter off (an
experiment build), and before and after the other changes here, so it lies
in the stored average itself. A dither of half a step either way, correct
for a store that rounds to the nearest, did not stop it (131.6 to 115.2):
the device's stores behave as rounding toward zero, which Vulkan allows **[I]**.

**Change.** `rt_half_dither.glsl` rounds a still view's averages to a whole
half-float step itself, up or down with the chance that keeps their expected
value, so the stored value is a half float exactly and the store's rounding
mode never applies. `tests/rtx_half_dither_tests.py` runs the shader's own
function against both of a store's roundings: a lit surface a shadow crosses
one sample in eight keeps 0.03% of its 2,048-sample mean where rounding to
the nearest loses 15.0% and rounding toward zero 27.8%. The same frozen view
now converges: 137.6, 138.5, 138.8, 139.0 (Live).

Two related denoiser defects were fixed with it:

- The first à-trous iteration's output replaced the history every frame, as
  SVGF does for a moving view. In a still view that fed the filter's result
  back into the average each frame, blurring it further every frame. A still
  view now keeps its plain average, and the filter's luminance stop takes
  that average's own variance (the samples' over their number), so it steps
  back as the picture settles.
- The ray's sub-pixel jitter during accumulation was left out of the
  motion vector, so a still view reprojected each pixel through the jitter
  and resampled its history between neighbours. The motion is now the
  surface's own; a moving view, which has no jitter, is unchanged.

The history length is capped at 2,048, the largest whole number a half float
holds; 4,096 was never reached.

## Shading fixes

- **`rtx_rt_mis 0` counted lamps and the sky twice in glossy reflections.**
  The reflection showed a q3map surface light's glow and the sky while the
  light sample kept them as well. Whether a reflection is traced and whether
  it shares lights by multiple importance sampling are now separate: without
  it the reflection leaves surface lights to the light sample, and the light
  sample leaves the sky to the reflection.
- **Glowing surfaces the light sampler does not hold went black in
  reflections.** Every glowing shader is marked as a light, but lights are
  built only from the world's own surfaces that pass the light build's checks.
  A mover's glowing texture, a surface the build left out, and every glowing
  surface under `rtx_rt_emissive_light 0` showed nothing in a reflection. A
  reflection now asks the light sampler's table for the very triangle it meets
  (bounds-checked) and shows the glow where no light stands for it. Bounces
  still leave a marked surface's glow out, so movers still light nothing (see
  below).
- **No reflection is traced where the shading normal faces away from the
  view.** Its weight's `1 / (N.V)` was taken at `1e-4` and produced fireflies
  at normal-mapped silhouettes; the specular light term already gave none.
- **Fresnel's `pow(1 - V.H, 5)` is clamped**, as `V.H` can round past 1.
- **A texture pack's `_specGloss` colour is decoded like the surface's own
  colour** when light is traced linear (`rtx_rt_lighting 3`,
  `rtx_rt_camera 1`); it set about five times too much specular there.
- **The frame number reaches the trace wrapped below 2^23.** As a float it
  stopped being exact after 2^24 frames (about 78 hours at 60 fps), when
  consecutive frames would share their random sequences and an odd indirect
  phase never came round. The denoiser's phase follows the same number.

## Light and material table fixes

- **The image-average cache is reset with the material table**
  (`RT_MaterialsFree`, which a new map, a restart and a lighting change all
  reach). It was reset only when the lights were built, after the new map's
  materials had read it, so an image at an address the last map used took
  that map's average and its glow. The lights now reuse the averages the
  materials read instead of decoding the images a second time.
- **A failed light build keeps the lighting mode and the sky's colour.**
  Clearing them made `rtx_rt_lighting 1` drop its lightmaps (a near-black
  world) and `3` its camera when the light grid could not be allocated.
- **The glowing surfaces' lights follow a remapped shader**, as their
  materials do.
- **A zero-area autosprite surface light is skipped** instead of becoming a
  point light at the world's origin.
- **A growing material table no longer drops motion history.** Each model's
  motion record took the table's growth count, so the first use of a shader
  in a frame made every model recorded before it look new. Records now take
  the table's rebuild count; the indices they hash keep their meaning while
  the table grows.

## Frame and lifetime fixes

- **Hardware backend: a dynamic BLAS is never updated from one the GPU did
  not build.** Builds are marked done as they are planned; a frame whose plan
  failed part-way, or whose recorded commands are thrown away (the raster
  geometry buffer grows, `vk_resize_geometry_buffer`), left the slot marked
  built and the next frame would update a structure that never existed. Both
  now abandon the slot's dynamic structures. The hardware path does not run
  on this machine's driver; this fix has build and SPIR-V evidence only.
- **A discarded frame no longer leaves its history, camera, tone-mapper clear
  and timestamps as if it had run** (`RT_FrameDiscarded`).
- **The texture table follows a replaced image view.** A cinematic or the web
  UI's image re-created at a new size destroyed the view the trace's table
  still held; the table is now rewritten when any image's view is replaced.
- **A world that fails to build is not retried every frame.** Each try waited
  for the GPU, re-extracted the world, logged a warning and drew the frame
  twice (the traced base pass, then all of it again by raster). A failure now
  waits 0.5 s before trying again, doubling up to about a minute, and the
  view is left to raster meanwhile (still tried, and stopped on, under
  `rtx_rt_require 1`). A new map, a restart or a setting the world is built
  for tries at once. The compute world's build arrays are freed when the
  build fails. A first attempt that ran out of the 32-bit address space while
  testing this round is why it retries rather than giving up.
- **A lighting change after a failed or dropped world rebuilds its
  materials.** A patch-shadow change in the same frame hid that a world had
  been built; the lighting change is now handled first, and a failed wait for
  the GPU keeps the change pending rather than dropping it.

## Faster tracing

- **Bounce light on the compute backend.** At the compute default, indirect
  divisor 2, one pixel in each 2 x 2 block traces a bounce each frame, so
  every warp waited on its few bouncing lanes. Each 8 x 8 workgroup now takes
  the pixels at one place in 8 x 8 blocks (`RT_TraceGroupDivisor`), so the
  frame's bouncing pixels fill whole workgroups; without a bounce the old
  layout is kept. On Quake III's `four` the bounce's share of the trace fell
  from 8.9 to 6.7 ms.
- **A glossy reflection's hit is lit as a bounce's is**: one light sample of
  four candidates. Only what a liquid shows through its interface keeps the
  full sixteen candidates and all three samplers.
- **Compute traversal's slab test** takes one fused multiply-add per slab,
  with the ray's origin scaled once per ray; the traversal fixture (32,000
  queries against a double-precision reference) passes unchanged.
- **An alpha test's texture lookup** skips the footprint arithmetic when there
  is no footprint.

Fixed views, compute backend, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4060 Laptop GPU, private
desktop, no input, isolated homes, read-only installations; GPU pass times
are medians of the periodic reports:

| View | Size | Trace before / after, ms | GPU total before / after, ms |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Quake III `four` demo, frozen | 1280 x 720 | 25.80 / 23.38 | 29.52 / 27.04 |
| RTCW SP `forest` | 800 x 600 | 21.73 / 20.02 | 23.53 / 21.83 |
| Quake III `q3dm1` | 800 x 600 | 10.33 / 9.71 | 11.98 / 11.36 |
| RTCW MP `mp_beach` | 800 x 600 | 12.25 / 12.05 | 13.65 / 13.45 |
| Medal of Honor `m1l1`, frozen | 1280 x 720 | 27.23-27.46 / 26.72 | 30.11-30.35 / 29.53 |

`forest`'s shaded ground keeps its brightness and noise (mean 6.85 / 6.86,
temporal deviation 0.632 / 0.629), and the other views' captures differ by
their frame noise only. The `four` baseline repeated to 0.01 ms.

Correction: the Quake III views loaded through the Steam install, whose
third-party `q3r-maps.pk3` replaces `q3dm7` (where `four` plays) and `q3dm1`
with its own versions. Both sides of each comparison loaded the same files,
and the darkening is a property of the history store, so the relative
results stand, but the Quake III rows are not retail-map evidence. Later
probes load the retail paks alone
([animated surfaces](2026-11-05-rtx-animated-surfaces.md#correction-to-the-november-5-round)).

## Diagnostics

`rtx_rt_perf_timing 1` now also reports the path tracer's CPU time split into
the models' extraction, the scene's upload and BVH or acceleration-structure
builds, and the rest, with the traced triangle counts of the dynamic, weapon
and body classes. Temporary timers on `m1l1`'s frozen truck ride (30,000
traced TIKI triangles) then split its 6 ms: the TIKI appender 1.1-1.4, the
compute BVH refit 2.4-2.8, the partition and the motion records about 0.4
each, the still view's hash 0.4, uploads 0.4. The GPU sets the frame rate
there (47 of 60 main-thread stack samples wait for it).

## Evidence

- Build: the Windows x86 RTX renderer with the shared build's strict
  warnings; all 97 shader variants compile, and `spirv-val` passes all 13
  embedded path-tracer modules, the ray-tracing pipeline's stages among them.
- Unit/Source: all 43 RTX test files pass (42 at HEAD plus the new dither
  fixture), with 17 other renderer test files, the shadow-projection script
  and the GLx and Vulkan folders' 234 tests. Three source assertions follow
  the new MIS and emission rules; the traversal fixture's shim gained `fma`.
- Live: the views above, on the frozen runtimes
  `.tmp/runtime/rtx-round-before` (HEAD) and `rtx-round-after`, with ray
  tracing required (`rtx_rt_require 1`) and the compute backend active.

## FnQ features

The path tracer is the FnQ feature, applied to each game's own lights,
shaders and models. No retail presentation replaces it and no baked light
feeds it.

## Not done

- A moving view's denoised light reads about 8% darker than the settled still
  view of the same frame on `four`; which of the two the raw trace's mean
  sides with could not be measured cleanly (the raw view's sparse ×4
  bounce samples clip in 8-bit captures) **[I]**.
- Glowing movers and models still light nothing: lights are built from the
  world alone, and a bounce leaves a marked glow to light sampling.
- With `rtx_rt_checkerboard 1` (or divisor 1), a surface revealed inside a
  skipped 8 x 8 tile has no bounce light until the tile is sampled.
- `rtx_rt_lighting 2`'s `_minlight` floor is applied to one noisy bounce
  sample before denoising, which biases it upward.
- A model's appearing, disappearing or level-of-detail change rebuilds its
  whole class's compute BVH (8.5 ms for 30,000 triangles in a benchmark of
  the builder); per-model trees would bound that frame spike. The global
  light pick's binary search, the motion record's 64-bit hash and two
  denoiser re-fetches are smaller CPU and GPU savings left open.
- The hardware ray-tracing backend has not run any of this.

## Unrelated issues found

- `RE_UploadCinematic` and Quake Live's web UI destroy an image's view without
  waiting for frames still in flight (raster renderers too).
- `RT_MaterialIndexForShader` returns material 0, another shader's, when
  translation fails.
- Every `RE_RemapShader` invalidates the whole traced scene (world, lights,
  light grid), and `vk_rt_invalidate` does nothing when its wait fails.
- `tr_backend.c`'s comment that a failed trace "is known before the trace ends
  the raster pass" is wrong for failures after the scene build; the fallback
  still draws, in the post-bloom pass.
