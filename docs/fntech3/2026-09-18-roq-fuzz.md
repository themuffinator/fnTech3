# Seeded corruption of installed RoQ movies

The [RoQ bounds checkpoint](2026-09-17-roq-bounds.md) checked movie framing,
dimensions, codebooks, VQ and motion reads, and recovery from nine synthetic
malformed movies. The cinematic rows of the ledger still asked for complete
malformed-codec and fuzz coverage. This record corrupts the installed movies
themselves, thousands of times, both in the shared decoder under
AddressSanitizer and in the running client. No engine code changed; the decoder
under test is `code/client/cl_cin.cpp` as committed at that checkpoint. The
[machine-readable record](2026-09-18-roq-fuzz.json) holds identities, seeds,
commands and per-movie results.

## Decoder under AddressSanitizer

[`scripts/fuzz_roq_decoder.py`](../../scripts/fuzz_roq_decoder.py) compiles the
decoder's actual bodies (chunk loop, codebook, VQ, quad set-up and audio
decoders, extracted as [`tests/roq_bounds_tests.py`](../../tests/roq_bounds_tests.py)
does) into a disposable Windows x86 harness built with MSVC's
AddressSanitizer. Each movie is read from its pak in memory and sent over
stdin; the harness derives its variants itself, so no corrupted copy of a
retail movie reaches the disk. Variant *n* draws from splitmix64 seeded with the
run seed and *n*, and *n* mod 4 picks the damage:

- **payload**: 1–32 random bytes inside chunk payloads, so the framing stays
  valid and the codebook, VQ and audio decoders see the damage;
- **anywhere**: 1–32 random bytes anywhere, file and chunk headers included;
- **header**: one chunk header's id, size, size by −16 to 16, or flags replaced;
- **structure**: a truncation, a chunk removed or duplicated, or up to 4 KiB
  zeroed.

Every second pair of variants plays looping, as `cinematic <name> 2` does. Each
plays the way `CIN_PlayCinematic` and `CIN_RunCinematic` drive the decoder,
until it is rejected, ends or has looped twice. The unmodified movie must play
to its end first. A variant fails on a sanitizer report, on a drawable frame
outside the fixed frame buffers, on a new frame at neither frame buffer, on an
audio block larger than the decode buffer, or on a decoder pass that does not
end within one call per eight bytes of input.

The three longest movies ran 100 variants each, with a second seed. All 7,300
variants pass, with no sanitizer report and no failed check.
Rejections name the decoder's own reasons, most often an invalid VQ payload or
motion range (2,165 in the 1,000-variant runs), an invalid or truncated chunk
header (956) and a truncated chunk payload (420). Removing or duplicating a
whole chunk keeps the framing valid, so those variants play to their end.

To show the harness finds the defects this decoder was fixed for, the same
40-variant run was pointed at `cl_cin.cpp` from before the bounds checkpoint.
The unmodified logo plays, and variant 37 then stops with an AddressSanitizer
global-buffer-overflow read in `blitVQQuad32fs`.

## Running client

[`scripts/probe_roq_faults.py`](../../scripts/probe_roq_faults.py) writes 60
seeded variants of one installed movie into an isolated home, half with payload
damage and half with damage anywhere, and plays them in one fnTech3 process with
`cinematic`. Every second one loops, and each starts 20 frames after the one
before, stopping it mid-play. Then the unmodified movie plays from the
installation, and the engine's own screenshot records it. Corrupted copies are
deleted afterwards and only their hashes kept.

| Profile | Movie | Played | Rejected | Refused at the header |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `q3` | `tier1.RoQ` | 57 | 3 | 0 |
| `rtcw-sp` | `menu.roq` | 58 | 2 | 0 |
| `rtcw-mp` | `mp_beach.roq` | 58 | 2 | 0 |

All three runs reach the final marker and exit normally, and each screenshot
shows the unmodified movie. Because each variant plays only 20 frames, most of
the damage lies beyond the part that ran; these runs check the client path and
recovery within one process, and the harness covers the decoding depth. The QL
installation ships no RoQ movies; the earlier checkpoint's synthetic malformed
movies remain its evidence. Runs are windowed on unswitched private desktops
with sound and mouse/joystick input disabled, and use console commands only.

## Remaining

The original engines were not run on these corruptions. Original visual and
timing parity, audible audio/video synchronisation, game-script fades and full
campaign movie transitions remain open, as does audio decoding with sound
enabled in the running client.
