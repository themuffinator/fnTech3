# Jedi Academy's zoom: turned pictures and ranged fog

Date: 2026-10-15. Profile: `jka-mp`. Platform: Windows 11, the Windows x86
build. Companion: [`2026-10-15-jka-zoom.json`](2026-10-15-jka-zoom.json).

The [live HUD check](2026-10-15-jka-hud-live-check.md) found that any zoom
stopped Academy's multiplayer client before its mask was drawn. The client
game sets the renderer's ranged fog (cgame import 216, `CG_R_SETRANGEFOG`)
every zoomed frame, and the host refused that import by name. The disruptor's
mask then turns its insert and ticks with import 215 (`CG_R_DRAWROTATEPIC2`),
and the rocket lock turns its wedges with import 214 (`CG_R_DRAWROTATEPIC`).
Both were refused too. This record measures retail `jamp.exe` 1.01's
handlers for all three and serves them through fnTech3's own HUD and fog
paths. Both zooms now draw live, with every piece registered with its
stretched mask at 21:9 and 5:4. No gate closes.

## Retail handlers (observed)

`jamp.exe` 1.01 (SHA-256 `e8062d39...ee28b`) dispatches client-game imports
at `0x412160`. The ordinal is checked against `0x15e`, mapped through the
byte table at `0x414638` and dispatched through the jump table at
`0x4142bc`.

- **214** (`0x412e5e`) pushes argument words 1 to 10 and calls the
  renderer's slot `[0x8af0a0]`, then returns 0. **215** (`0x412e98`) does
  the same through `[0x8af0a4]`. The client copies the renderer's 45-word
  table from `GetRefAPI` (`0x4a3230`, table at `0x1309b88`) to `0x8af048`, so
  the two slots are `RE_RotatePic` (`0x491760`) and `RE_RotatePic2`
  (`0x491810`).
- Each queues a 44-byte render command: id 3 or 4, the shader pointer, then
  x, y, w, h, s1, t1, s2, t2 and the angle as stored. Nothing is converted or
  checked except the handle. A handle below zero or past the shader count
  prints a warning (`0x554504`) and takes the default shader. The command
  is dropped when the buffer lacks `0x30` bytes.
- The backend dispatches command 3 to `RB_RotatePic` (`0x48b7c0`) and 4 to
  `RB_RotatePic2` (`0x48b8b0`). Both draw with fixed-function GL in the 2D
  projection, the `glOrtho` 640x480 screen. They set the 2D colour,
  `glPushMatrix`, translate, then `glRotatef(angle, 0, 0, 1)`, which turns
  clockwise on the y-down screen. One `GL_QUADS` follows, then
  `glPopMatrix`.
  - `RB_RotatePic` translates to (x + w, y), the top-right corner, and draws
    (-w, 0), (0, 0), (0, h), (-w, h). Its texture coordinates are (s1, t1),
    (s2, t1), (s2, t2) and (s1, t2).
  - `RB_RotatePic2` translates to (x, y), the centre, and draws (-w/2, -h/2),
    (w/2, -h/2), (w/2, h/2), (-w/2, h/2). The constants at `0x5662d0` and
    `0x565f90` are -0.5 and 0.5. Texture coordinates are as above.
  - Both bind only the first stage's first image. `RB_RotatePic2` sets that
    stage's state bits, skips a shader with no stage, and restores state
    `0x10065` after. `RB_RotatePic` sets no state at all.
- **216** (`0x412ed2`) stores argument word 1 at `0x100d830` and returns 0.
  Nothing else writes that word, so it lasts until the client game changes
  it, across maps too. The far clip is not touched: `0x100d834`, the world's
  `distanceCull` that import 309 reads (`0x4132cf`), is only read here.
- The only reader is the shader stage setup at `0x4aed70`. It applies when the
  surface's fog is the world-covering one or the extra slot (`0xbc`/`0xb4`
  of the world). The shader must have a fog pass and `r_drawfog` must be
  exactly 2. `r_drawfog` is registered with default "2" and flag `0x200` at
  `0x4a2786`, and stored at `0xfe30dc`.
  - With the word at zero, GL fog is `GL_EXP2`, with density from the fog's
    `depthForOpaque` (fog + `0x30`).
  - Otherwise it is `GL_LINEAR`, ending at `distanceCull`. A negative word
    starts it at minus the word. A positive word keeps `depthForOpaque` as
    the start unless `distanceCull` minus that start is less than the word.
    The start is then `distanceCull` minus the word, but never under 16.0
    (`0x56620c`).
- The retail client game (`assets3.pk3` `cgamex86.dll`, SHA-256
  `e5d55258...140d`) holds one trap wrapper for each: `push 214` at
  `0x3005e9f6`, `push 215` at `0x3005ea46` and `push 216` at `0x3005ea65`.

The OpenJK reference's `cg_view.c` and `cg_draw.c` agree with the client
game's use. It sends `fov_x * 64` every zoomed frame, 0 once on leaving
the zoom, and minus the worldspawn `fogstart` every frame when a map sets
one. No installed Academy map sets `fogstart`. Four multiplayer maps
compile a world-covering fog: `ctf2`, `ctf4`, `ffa2` and `ffa4`. Their
surfaces carry its index, so Fn's fog volume pass already draws it.

## What fnTech3 does

- **214 and 215** go through the HUD path. The host
  (`client_jka_cgame_host.hpp`) builds the turned quad in the virtual 640x480
  screen with `code/compat/jka_draw_adapter.hpp`, using retail's pivots,
  corner order and rotation sense. The picture's unturned rectangle goes
  through `CL_HudFromVirtual` and `CL_HudAdjustPic`, so the HUD rules place
  it like any picture. The turned corners then take that rectangle's scale
  and offset, and `re.DrawQuad` draws them in the active Fn renderer with
  the whole shader. Two results follow. A stretched picture is turned in
  640x480 and stretched after, as retail's screen did, so the disruptor's
  insert stays registered with its stretched mask. A uniformly placed one,
  such as a rocket-lock wedge, simply turns. Held text is drawn first, a
  capture that hides the HUD hides these too, and a picture without area
  draws nothing. A non-finite word stops with an error, as the host's other
  records do.
- **216** goes to Fn's fog. The renderers gain `SetJKARangedFog`, which
  stores the range in the loaded world. `RB_CalcFogTexCoords` and
  `RB_CalcFogProgramParms`, in GLx, Vulkan and RTX, rescale the fog volume
  pass for the world-covering volume. The pass then rises linearly from
  retail's start to full fog at `distanceCull`, with the start computed as
  retail does (`tr_jka_ranged_fog.h`). Fn's volume pass is already linear,
  opaque at `depthForOpaque`, so the ramp needs only a new scale and an
  offset. Volumes with brushes of their own and every other profile are
  unchanged. The far clip is unchanged, as in retail.

Differences kept on purpose, all presentation (invariant 11):

- Fn draws the turned pictures' full shaders (stages, blending and
  animation) where retail bound one image.
- Fn has no `r_drawfog`. The range applies to Fn's fog volume pass whatever
  that cvar would have been. Retail's unranged global fog was GL `EXP2`;
  Fn's is its own linear volume fog.
- A new world starts with no range. Retail kept the word across maps until
  the client game reset it.
- A ramp that would end before it starts, a negative start past the limit,
  gets one unit where retail's GL linear fog divided by zero.

## Captures (observed)

Runtime: `git archive` of `30c1286d` with this slice overlaid, built with
the developer options, strict warnings, client and GLx, Vulkan and RTX
renderers. Copied to `.tmp/runtime/jka-zoom`; hashes are in the companion.

desktop, `net_enabled 0`, `s_initsound 0`, an isolated home with an empty
`jampconfig.cfg`, no input. Captures are the engine's `screenshotPNG`, and
`cl_hudDump` is recorded. The installation and the runtime were unchanged
after every run. The client game was told its screen is the 4:3 frame,

Every piece each zoom drew is a dump group, run through the shipped rules
(the companion lists them all):

- **Binoculars.** `binMask` (0,0,640,480), `binocularWindow` (34,48,570,362),
  `binCircle` (82,94,16,16), `binSideArrow` (4,201,16,16) at this zoom
  level, `binTopTri` (307,40,26,30) and `binocularNumOverlay`
  (212,367,200,28) take `binocular_zoom`. The black box behind the numbers,
  `white` (212,367,200,40), and the tick dots, `white` 6x6 at y 376, take
  `binocular_strip`. The chunky digits, `gfx/2d/numbers/c_*` 24x14 at y 374,
  take `binocular_numbers`. All of them stretch with the mask. In the
  captures the window sits in the mask's opening, and the arrow, circle and
  strip sit in their places on its frame, at 21:9 and 5:4.
- **Disruptor.** `cropCircle2` (the mask) and `cropCircle` (the turned
  insert, unturned rectangle 0,0,640,480) take `disruptor_zoom`. The
  `insertTick` group, ticks turned about their own centres on radius 190,
  takes `disruptor_ticks`. At 21:9 the insert's rings are ellipses aligned
  with the stretched mask's, and the green ammunition ticks run along its
  rim. At 5:4 all are near circles.
- The crosshair and the rest of the HUD (health, armour, force, ammo,
  leader icon) keep their own rules over both masks.

The strip scrolls with the view's yaw. The first run with these rules
(`zoom-binoculars-1680`) drew a tick dot centred at x 488. That was past the
strip region's first right edge at x 480, so `right_hud` took it. In the
reference the dots reach x 545 and the digits x 521 (`155 + i*10 + off*10`
with three digits; `245 + (i-1)*10 + off*10`). The regions were widened to
x 560 and 520 before the runs above.

Ranged fog: on `ffa4` and `ctf4` both the unzoomed and the zoomed shot drew
the world fog with no error. The scenes are close, so the captures do not
show the ramp itself. Its effect is established by the production fog
functions under test (below), not by these images.

## Rules

`pkg/games/jka-mp/base/fntech3-hud.json` gains, ahead of the position rules:

- `rocket_lock` and `rocket_lock_icon` (`gfx/2d/wedge`, `gfx/2d/lock`):
  centre, middle. They stay on the target the client game projected, even
  near an edge where `left_column` or `upper_right` would take them.
- `binocular_zoom` (`gfx/2d/bin*`), `disruptor_zoom` (`gfx/2d/crop*`, which
  includes the charge bar `crop_charge`) and `disruptor_ticks`
  (`gfx/2d/insertTick`): stretch.
- `binocular_numbers` (`gfx/2d/numbers/c_*`, centre in x 40-520 and y
  372-390) and `binocular_strip` (`white`, centre in x 130-560 and y
  376-390): stretch. No dump of normal play holds a `white` or chunky
  digit group there.

`cl_hudAspect 0` still draws everything stretched, as retail did.

## Tests

- `tests/jka_zoom_tests.py` (new):
  - The ranged-fog span and scale run in C and C++, covering every retail
    branch, the 16-unit floor, non-finite words and the one-unit guard.
  - The turned-picture geometry is checked against retail's
    translate-then-`glRotatef` corners for the rocket lock's eight wedges,
    the disruptor's ticks and a quarter turn, along with empty and
    non-finite input and the stretched and uniform placements.
  - The production host cases for 214, 215 and 216 run against a stand-in
    HUD and renderer. They check the queue flush, a hidden HUD, refusals,
    the dropped renderer, and fog words of `fov_x * 64`, 0 and an override.
  - Each renderer's production `RE_SetJKARangedFog` and
    `RB_CalcFogProgramParms` run with the fog factor checked at chosen
    depths: the world-covering volume's ramp, a brushed volume untouched,
    other profiles, no world limit, and non-finite words.
- `tests/hud_aspect_tests.py` gains Academy samples at 2560x1080 for every
  piece of both zooms and the rocket lock, plus a white picture and a chunky
  digit outside the strip that keep their old rules, and the strip's
  farthest dot and digit. At 900x720 it gains the mask, window and circle.

- The ranged fog's ramp is not shown by an image comparison, and no retail

- Siege's radar also uses import 215, which a siege round reached and found
